OnLive Expo: idea evaluation (v2)

Updated for the cheaper plan, the walkable 3D demo and the "free pilot with a big company" strategy.

One line: a walkable online expo where companies rent a booth for a small monthly fee, stand their products on stage and receive enquiries directly. One large "anchor partner" gets the central hall free in exchange for letting you use their name in marketing, once they agree in writing.

1. Verdict, thinking like a business advisor

Yes, with conditions.

  • As an open "virtual expo for everyone": probably not. It hits the cold-start problem (no visitors, so no exhibitors, so no visitors). Many virtual expos faded after 2021, and directories such as IndiaMART and TradeIndia already own buyer intent.
  • As an anchor-led, invite-only expo for one niche: it can work. A big company already has hundreds of vendors and dealers. If it invites them, the exhibitors arrive on day one, and each exhibitor invites its own buyers. That is how you solve cold start without ad spend.
  • The 3D hall is the hook, not the moat. It makes the pitch memorable and visitors stay longer, but it is easy to copy. Your real moat is the anchor relationship, the vendor network around it and the lead data. Keep the directory and search mode, because serious buyers want to find a supplier in seconds, not walk.

2. The pitch to a big company: free pilot, in exchange for their name

What you give (all free for 6 months)

  • The Anchor Hall: the tower at the centre of the expo with their name, banner wall and a stage slot.
  • Free listings for up to 50 of their vendors or dealers for 90 days, with your team doing the onboarding.
  • A monthly report: visitors, enquiries, vendor sign-ups.
  • No IT work on their side. It is a link, not an integration.

What you ask for

  1. A short signed pilot letter.
  2. Written permission to say "Anchor partner: [Company]" and show their logo on your site and outreach, in wording they approve.
  3. An introduction to whoever runs vendor development, channel partners or MSME programmes.
  4. A short quote or case study after 90 days if it went well.

Why they might say yes

  • It costs nothing and needs no integration.
  • It gives them a modern, visible way to support vendors and MSMEs.
  • They see which of their vendors are active and which buyers show up.
  • A pilot is easy to stop, so the risk is small.

Why they might say no, and what to do about it

Objection Your answer
"Brand risk if the hall looks empty" Private, invite-only launch. Public only once 20+ booths are live.
"Where does visitor data go?" You are the data handler. Consent box on every form, data deleted on request, monthly report only shows totals.
"Procurement and legal take months" One-page letter, no money moves, no data integration.
"We will build our own" Offer your hall as the pilot. Building takes them a year. You are ready now.
"Why you?" Show the live demo, the 12 plots and your plan to fill them with their vendors.

Using their name for marketing: the rules

  1. Written permission first. Do not put their name or logo anywhere public until the letter is signed. Using it earlier is a trademark and misrepresentation risk, and one wrong post can end the deal.
  2. Agree the exact wording. "Pilot partner" is fine. "Endorsed by", "invested in by" or "used by all their vendors" are not, unless they say so.
  3. Give them approval and a kill switch. They approve each use and can ask you to remove their name within 48 hours.
  4. The demo is a placeholder. The Anchor Hall in the demo shows "Your Brand Here". The preview box lets you type their name for a private meeting only.
  5. Never claim results they have not confirmed.
  6. Get a lawyer to read the one-page letter. This is business advice, not legal advice.

Where their name earns the most: the strip at the top of your site, the invitation email to their vendors ("invited by [Company]"), a launch press note, a LinkedIn post and the 90-day case study.

Who to approach, in order

  1. A mid-size regional leader with 100 to 1,000 vendors or dealers. Faster decisions, same proof.
  2. An industry association or chamber. They have members, like free, and can announce you.
  3. A large company, using the first two as proof.

Good targets are firms with big vendor or dealer networks: agri inputs and machinery, auto components, FMCG distribution, building materials, bank and NBFC MSME teams, B2B logistics and payments. Warm introductions beat cold email. Plan for 30 to 50 conversations to get one yes.

3. Cheaper plan

Prices

Plan Before Now What the exhibitor gets
Starter ₹1,999 / month ₹499 / month Small booth, 2 products, enquiry form
Growth ₹5,999 / month ₹1,499 / month LED screen, 3 products, meetings, lead list
Premier ₹14,999 / month ₹3,999 / month Large booth with arch, banners, team inbox, report
Top spot ₹2,999 / week ₹999 / week Gold beacon, shown first, max 3 per category

Founding offer: first 90 days free, launch price locked for 12 months, "Founding exhibitor" badge, limited to the first 50 companies. Offer annual prepay (for example Starter ₹4,990, two months free) once they see leads.

Why this price works

  • ₹499 a month is a spend a small owner can approve without asking anyone.
  • The free period removes the "will anyone visit?" fear. They pay only after they have seen enquiries.
  • The top spot is cheap enough to sell often, and capped per category so visitors still trust the order.

First 3 months in cash: roughly ₹1,000 to ₹3,000, plus a lawyer's look at the pilot letter. Free tiers change, so check current limits before relying on them. The 3D hall is drawn with code, not heavy 3D files, so pages stay light and bandwidth stays low.

5. Risks

  • Cold start. Reduced by the anchor, not removed. Do not open publicly until 20+ booths are live.
  • Depends on one anchor. Aim for a second partner or an association within 6 months.
  • Gimmick versus buyer intent. Measure how many enquiries come from the hall versus the directory. Keep both.
  • Pay-to-top hurts trust. Label it "Top spot", cap it, rotate it.
  • Fake or low-quality companies. GST and email-domain check before the "Verified" badge.
  • Spam leads. Rate limits, captcha, one enquiry per visitor per booth per day.
  • Privacy. Consent box on every form (included in the demo), delete on request, follow India's DPDP Act.
  • Competition. IndiaMART, TradeIndia, LinkedIn and event tools. Your edge: one niche, one anchor, live events.

6. First 90 days

When Do
Weeks 1 to 2 Pick one niche. List 40 target companies. Ask for 10 warm introductions. Get the pilot letter reviewed.
Weeks 3 to 6 Run demos. Aim for one anchor (regional leader or association). Collect their vendor list.
Weeks 7 to 10 Onboard 20 to 50 vendors for free. Build login, uploads and the lead inbox (MVP).
Weeks 11 to 13 Launch publicly with a 3-day live event. Send the first monthly report. Start paid plans for exhibitors who got leads.

7. What the demo includes

  • A walkable expo ground: WASD, arrow keys or tap to move, scroll to zoom.
  • 9 sample booths in three sizes, each with products standing on stage, plus 3 empty plots.
  • Click or walk up to a booth: products you can look at closer, meeting slots, an enquiry form with consent box.
  • Passport with stamps, coins, quests, points and ranks.
  • Build your booth: pick name, colour, plan and products, and it rises into the hall instantly, with sample analytics.
  • Anchor Hall in the centre, with a private preview box for the partner name.
  • Directory and search for buyers who would rather not walk.
  • Plans page with the cheaper prices.

All companies are fictional. Nothing is sent to a server; enquiries and your booth are saved only in your browser.

8. Roadmap

  1. Demo (this zip): pitch tool.

  3. Growth: analytics, live events, meeting booking, CRM export.
  4. Scale: white-label expos for associations and cities, mobile app, buyer and seller matching.
